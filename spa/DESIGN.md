# DESIGN — identity, and the record of one reversal

## Current identity

A commodity-analytics **terminal**: true black, amber (`#ffb000`) as the single accent on
headline figures and section headers, warm-white monospace for data, functional green/red for
direction, everything flat (no rounded corners), high density. The map's heat ramp runs
green → amber → red, so cheap reads green and expensive reads red, as on a trading screen.

The **signature element is the zonal map**: seven bidding zones that couple at night and split
at peak. It is the one thing the subject provides and a single-price market cannot have.

## The reversal (2026-07-16)

An earlier version of this document made a binding rule of the opposite. It read:

> The visual identity must be **autonomous from the references studied**, especially
> `lptva/gb-power-dashboard`. Kinship is allowed **only at the methodological level** […] not at
> the visual level. Do **not** reuse the dark-terminal + heat-ramp look. […] For the portfolio,
> the reading to avoid is "the GB project ported to Italy".

The dashboard was then reskinned into precisely that look. Rather than quietly restate the
principle to fit the outcome, the decision is recorded:

**Reversed, and why.**

1. **The concern is answered better by attribution than by palette.** The rule existed to avoid
   being read as an undisclosed port. The README now states plainly what was adopted from
   `gb-power-dashboard` — architecture, provenance discipline, publish gate, incremental ETL,
   per-panel isolation. A different colour scheme would not have made the debt smaller; it
   would have made it less visible. Disclosure is the honest instrument here, not distance.
2. **The terminal idiom is a genre, not a signature.** Dark screens with amber figures are the
   vernacular of commodity analytics and predate both projects. Avoiding a convention because a
   reference also uses it is not originality.
3. **The distinctiveness is in the subject and the method**, not the styling: the Italian zonal
   structure and its spreads, the supply-side residual, the withdrawal of the distributed-PV
   estimator, the gas leg. Those do not change with a palette.

**What was given up.** The earlier document argued for an identity grounded in something
specifically Italian — the vernacular of Terna/GME system reports, an institutional palette.
Three alternative directions were prototyped under that principle and have been removed along
with it. That road was not taken, and the current look is closer to the reference than that
principle allowed. Stated here so the choice is visible rather than implied.

## Rules that still hold

- One signature element, loud; everything else quiet.
- Amber is an accent, not a wash: headline numbers and headers only. A screen that is all
  accent has none.
- Colour carries meaning or it does not appear: green/red is direction, the heat ramp is price
  level, cyan is the secondary driver. No decorative colour.
- Provenance badges are never suppressed for tidiness. They are the point.
